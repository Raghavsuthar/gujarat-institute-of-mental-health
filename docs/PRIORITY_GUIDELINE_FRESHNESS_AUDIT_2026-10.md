# Priority Clinical Guideline Freshness Audit — Phase 1

**Checked:** 2026-10-25  
**Scope:** Public guideline landing/recommendation pages for five priority conditions. This is a source-freshness triage, not a full evidence synthesis, claim-by-claim clinical review, or India-specific prescribing guideline.

## Summary

The existing condition evidence registry should be maintained with explicit dates and source types. The checks below show why a URL being present is not enough: guidelines can be old but still active, recently updated, or subject to a recent surveillance decision.

## Content safeguards for the next revision

- Do not copy treatment algorithms from UK guidance into Indian patient-facing content as if they were universal prescriptions. Use them to support general education, and have a qualified clinician check local applicability.
- Do not include drug doses or individualized medication instructions in public-facing condition summaries.
- For bipolar disorder, distinguish mania, bipolar depression, and long-term relapse prevention; avoid implying that one medication strategy fits every phase.
- For OCD, describe exposure and response prevention (ERP) as a structured therapy provided by a suitably trained professional. Avoid framing reassurance or compulsive rituals as the recommended self-help response.
- For PTSD, use trauma-informed language, avoid implying that everyone exposed to trauma develops PTSD, and avoid recommending unstructured self-exposure.
- For ADHD, avoid diagnosing from an online checklist or rating scale; note that symptoms and impairment require professional assessment and context.
- For schizophrenia, include recovery-oriented care, physical-health monitoring, family/carer support when appropriate, and urgent support guidance without stigmatizing language.
